A coat can look tidy on top while tangles develop underneath. Prevention starts with a gentle, repeatable check that reaches the areas your dog's coat tends to knot. The aim is comfortable skin and manageable hair, not finishing a brushing session at any cost.

## Match the routine to the coat

A short, smooth coat and a long or curly coat do not need the same tools or schedule. USDA guidance makes coat type and length part of deciding how often to groom. Ask your groomer or veterinary team to demonstrate the tool and technique that fit your dog.

Before buying a brush, write down the job: removing loose hair, checking for tangles or maintaining a particular coat type. A tool sold as powerful or fast is not automatically suitable. Our [grooming guide](/pet-guides/grooming-and-care) explains how to introduce the routine and prepare a stable working area.

## Check beyond the visible top layer

VCA highlights areas such as behind the ears, under the legs and around the groin where tangles can be missed. Look gently and stop if your dog resists or seems sore. Watch for trapped debris after outdoor activity, too.

Work through a small area rather than dragging a brush over the whole body repeatedly. If the top looks smooth but a suitable comb catches underneath, do not force it through. Note the location so a professional can assess it if needed.

Make an individual checklist after the first grooming demonstration. A household with a curly-coated dog may need a different list from one with a short-coated dog; copying someone else's shopping basket is less useful than learning your own dog's coat.

## Separate a small tangle from a mat that needs help

A mild tangle that releases with gentle grooming is different from a dense clump pulling against the skin. ASPCA advises professional help for mats that do not brush out easily and warns against cutting mats with scissors.

Stop if the area is painful, tight, extensive or close enough to the skin that you cannot see what you are doing. Skin can be caught inside a mat. Seek veterinary care for redness, sores, discharge or other signs of irritation; a cosmetic haircut does not address a skin problem.

## Make the session easy to end

Set out the tools before inviting your dog over. Choose a nonslip surface and a position that lets the dog stand or lie comfortably. Start with an easy area and pause frequently. If the dog repeatedly turns away, stiffens or tries to leave, reduce the task or stop.

Do not turn a coat check into a restraint contest. Ask the veterinary team about pain or handling concerns and how to build a more comfortable routine. A brief successful check gives you useful information even when the whole coat is not finished.

## Plan around bathing and wet outings

Existing tangles can become harder to manage after bathing. Check before the bath and get advice about mats rather than soaking them and hoping they will loosen. After a wet outing, follow a suitable drying and grooming plan for your dog's coat.

The [rainy-weather paw-care guide](/pet-guides/rainy-season-dog-paw-care-miami) covers the separate task of checking feet after walks. Avoid rubbing fragile or tangled hair aggressively with a towel.

## Leave a useful note for the next appointment

Record where knots recur, which tool you used and whether your dog showed discomfort. Ask the groomer to demonstrate one manageable section and explain when to return. Inspect brushes for damaged pins or rough edges using our [product-safety guidance](/product-safety).

A maintainable plan may include professional grooming plus short checks at home. Preventing a small problem from becoming a painful one is more valuable than achieving a particular hairstyle.
