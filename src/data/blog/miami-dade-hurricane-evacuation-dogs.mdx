A hurricane evacuation with a dog in Miami-Dade is easier to manage when you know **where your dog can go, what the County currently requires at a pet-friendly evacuation center, how you will transport the dog, and which supplies and records need to leave with you**.

This article focuses on the **Miami-Dade evacuation process**. For the broader emergency kit, shelter-in-place routine, identification checklist, and post-storm safety steps, use our main [Hurricane Preparedness for Dogs](/pet-guides/hurricane-preparedness-for-dogs) guide as the pillar resource.

## Start by knowing whether your home may need to evacuate

Miami-Dade County uses evacuation orders and storm-surge planning information based on the specific threat. Pet owners in an **evacuation zone, unsafe structure, or trailer/mobile home** are among those the County encourages to use pet-friendly evacuation options when they are activated.

Do not wait for a storm to be close before figuring out the basics. Before hurricane season or early in the season:

- know whether your home is in a storm-surge planning zone;
- know how to check current evacuation orders;
- save Miami-Dade County emergency information and 311 contact options;
- identify at least one family/friend or pet-friendly lodging backup outside the immediate evacuation area;
- make sure your dog can travel safely in the vehicle, crate, carrier, or restraint system you plan to use.

CDC hurricane guidance similarly recommends identifying shelters, pet-friendly lodging, or an out-of-town friend or relative **before** an evacuation is underway.

## Do not hard-code one shelter into your family plan

Miami-Dade County says pet-friendly evacuation centers are **announced as needed** during emergencies. That is important: a facility used during one storm may not be the correct destination during the next event.

When an evacuation is announced, check the current County list of open evacuation centers and pet-friendly options. Miami-Dade also directs residents to current emergency information through its website and 311.

A good plan therefore has two layers:

1. **a known process** for finding the currently open pet-friendly center; and
2. **a backup destination** such as family, friends, or pet-friendly lodging outside the affected area.

That is more resilient than printing one old shelter address and assuming it will always be active.

## Know the current Miami-Dade pet-friendly center requirements

Miami-Dade's current pet-preparedness page lists several requirements for pet-friendly evacuation centers. Before relying on a center, review the County page again because emergency requirements can change.

At the time this guide was prepared, the County says pet owners should be ready to provide or do the following:

- show proof of residency within an evacuation zone, such as photo identification plus a recent utility bill;
- present medical and current vaccination records for each pet;
- have current rabies vaccination documentation;
- for dogs, have a visible Miami-Dade County dog license as required by the County;
- bring supplies for both the people and pets in the household;
- keep the pet in a crate, on a leash, or otherwise controlled;
- have at least one family member remain at the evacuation center with the pet;
- comply with the County's current household pet limit and shelter instructions.

The County currently states a **limit of four pets per household** for the pet-friendly evacuation-center process. Verify that limit again before a specific evacuation because shelter operations are event-dependent.

## Prepare the records before the storm enters the forecast

Records become much harder to organize when you are also boarding windows, fueling a vehicle, moving outdoor items, and watching evacuation updates.

Keep a waterproof or protected document set containing:

- vaccination records;
- rabies documentation;
- Miami-Dade dog license information;
- microchip number and registry information;
- veterinary contact information;
- medication names and instructions;
- recent photos of your dog;
- proof of ownership when available;
- your own identification and local residency documentation.

Store digital copies securely as a backup, but do not assume your phone, internet connection, or battery will be reliable during an emergency.

## Make the carrier or restraint familiar before evacuation day

A crate or carrier is easier to use during an emergency when the dog has already experienced it during calm days.

Miami-Dade requires owners to maintain control of pets at evacuation centers. CDC emergency guidance also recommends preparing carriers and labeling them with the pet owner's contact information.

Before hurricane season gets active:

- confirm the carrier is the correct size and still closes securely;
- place it somewhere the dog can investigate during normal life;
- practice short periods inside it when appropriate;
- practice moving the carrier to the vehicle;
- check the leash, harness, collar, clips, and attachment points;
- make sure identification is current.

## Build the evacuation kit around what must travel with the dog

Your dog's hurricane kit should be portable enough to leave with you. The general [Hurricane Preparedness for Dogs](/pet-guides/hurricane-preparedness-for-dogs) article covers the full checklist; for evacuation specifically, prioritize what supports transport and sheltering.

A practical evacuation set includes:

- food in appropriate containers;
- drinking water;
- bowls or portable feeding/hydration products;
- medications and written instructions;
- leash, harness, and collar with current ID;
- crate or carrier when appropriate;
- vaccination and medical records;
- waste bags and cleanup supplies;
- basic first-aid supplies;
- a familiar blanket or small comfort item;
- recent photos and emergency contact information.

Do not pack the pet kit so deeply in the vehicle that you cannot reach water, medication, records, or restraint equipment during the trip.

## Plan the vehicle before the evacuation route becomes crowded

A hurricane evacuation is not the time to discover that the carrier does not fit where you thought it would or that all the luggage blocks access to the dog.

Do a dry run:

1. load the carrier or planned restraint setup;
2. decide where the emergency kit rides;
3. keep water and cleanup supplies accessible;
4. confirm every household member still fits safely;
5. identify fuel and charging needs;
6. know more than one reasonable route while still following official directions during the actual emergency.

CDC hurricane guidance says to follow routes recommended by emergency workers and never drive through flooded areas.

## If you evacuate to family, friends, or a hotel, confirm the pet plan directly

A pet-friendly evacuation center is not the only option. CDC recommends identifying alternatives such as friends or relatives, boarding facilities, veterinary resources, or pet-friendly lodging.

Before depending on a private destination, confirm:

- pets are actually permitted;
- any size or number restrictions;
- whether your dog's crate/carrier is needed indoors;
- where bathroom breaks can happen safely;
- whether medications or refrigeration are required;
- how long you could realistically remain there if returning home is delayed.

Do not assume a hotel that is normally pet-friendly will have rooms available once a large evacuation begins.

## Service animals are handled differently from pets

Miami-Dade states that **service animals are permitted at all types of hurricane or disaster shelters**, including general population shelters and special-needs shelters.

That is different from the rules for household pets. When planning for a service animal, follow the current County shelter guidance applicable to the person and service animal rather than treating the animal as a pet-shelter admission.

## If you stay home, keep the evacuation option ready anyway

Some households may be instructed or choose to shelter at home when it is appropriate and permitted by local officials. Miami-Dade recommends keeping pets together with the household in a safe area and having the dog secured with a crate/carrier or other appropriate control.

Even if you intend to stay:

- keep the pet kit ready;
- keep the carrier/leash accessible;
- keep identification on the dog;
- continue monitoring official instructions;
- be prepared to leave if authorities order evacuation or the home becomes unsafe.

A plan should be able to switch from **shelter in place** to **evacuate** without starting over from zero.

## After an evacuation order, use current information—not old screenshots

Storm details change. Center openings change. Bus pickup sites can be activated. Routes can flood or become blocked.

When Miami-Dade issues current evacuation instructions:

- verify the open pet-friendly centers;
- verify current admission requirements;
- confirm your evacuation zone/order status;
- follow official transportation and route guidance;
- use County emergency channels and 311 when clarification is needed.

Our guide can help you prepare the system, but **the County's current emergency information is the source of truth during an active storm**.

## A Miami-Dade dog evacuation checklist

Before hurricane season:

- [ ] Know your storm-surge/evacuation planning information.
- [ ] Keep your dog's rabies, vaccination, license, microchip, and medical information current.
- [ ] Practice the carrier or transport setup.
- [ ] Identify family/friend/lodging backups.
- [ ] Pack a portable dog emergency kit.

When a storm may affect Miami-Dade:

- [ ] Recheck medications, food, water, and records.
- [ ] Fuel or charge the vehicle.
- [ ] Confirm current County evacuation information.
- [ ] Verify which pet-friendly evacuation centers are actually open.
- [ ] Load the dog's supplies where they remain accessible.

If an evacuation order applies to you:

- [ ] Take your dog with you.
- [ ] Follow official routes and instructions.
- [ ] Keep the dog controlled with the appropriate leash/carrier/restraint.
- [ ] Bring the current records and supplies required by your destination.

## The most useful evacuation plan is one you can actually execute

A hurricane plan for a dog in Miami-Dade does not need to predict which shelter will open months in advance. It needs to make sure you can **find the current option quickly, prove what the shelter requires, transport your dog safely, and leave with the supplies and records you already prepared**.

That local planning layer works together with our broader [Hurricane Preparedness for Dogs](/pet-guides/hurricane-preparedness-for-dogs) pillar so your household is ready whether officials tell you to evacuate or shelter in place.
