A successful boarding handoff begins before the bag is packed. The important question is whether the care arrangement can meet your dog's needs, including quiet rest, meals, medication and help if something changes. A useful checklist records answers, not just objects to bring.

## Confirm requirements before booking the stay

Ask the facility for its current written health and admission policy. Bring those requirements to your veterinarian early enough to discuss records, vaccine timing and your dog's individual risks. Do not assume that another facility's policy applies or that a last-minute vaccination satisfies the requested lead time.

AAHA's vaccination guidance supports a plan based on the individual dog and exposure. A boarding policy is not a personalized medical prescription, and vaccination does not mean all infection risk disappears. Tell the facility and veterinary team about new coughing, vomiting, diarrhea or other illness before arrival.

## Ask how a normal day actually works

Request a description of sleeping arrangements, bathroom breaks, exercise, feeding and supervision. Ask whether group play is optional and what happens if your dog needs a quieter setting. A dog that enjoys a familiar playmate may not enjoy a large unfamiliar group.

Useful questions include:

- Who checks the dogs overnight, and how often?
- How are meals separated and intake recorded?
- How is a dog showing illness separated from others?
- Can staff accommodate mobility or handling needs?
- What would cause staff to call me or seek veterinary care?

Record the answers in one place. If a service your dog needs is unavailable, resolve that before committing to the stay.

## Test the plan when practical

AAHA encourages evaluating the environment, and VCA discusses shorter trial stays before a longer absence. Ask whether a suitable introductory visit or brief stay is possible. This is a chance to learn about the arrangement, not a guarantee that every future stay will feel the same.

Share what helps your dog rest and what increases discomfort. Our [home comfort guide](/pet-guides/comfort-at-home) can help you describe familiar routines clearly. Ask which bedding or toys are allowed and whether they suit your dog's chewing habits.

## Pack instructions with the supplies

Bring the usual food, clearly identified portions and enough for the planned stay plus a reasonable delay. Keep medication in its labeled container and provide the veterinarian's current instructions. Confirm that staff can perform the required administration; do not assume an extra service is included.

Add a recent photograph, identification details, your contact information and a backup decision-maker. Check the [microchip and ID routine](/pet-guides/dog-microchip-id-emergency-preparedness) before departure. Tell staff about dietary restrictions and items the dog must not receive.

## Set emergency decisions in writing

Ask how the facility obtains veterinary help and handles transport. Provide the regular veterinarian's details and discuss written authorization for urgent care. Agree on whom to contact if you are unreachable and how payment or spending decisions will be handled.

Do not leave staff to interpret an ambiguous instruction such as “call if anything seems wrong.” Ask for the facility's escalation process and explain any dog-specific signs your veterinary team has told you to watch for.

## Make pickup part of the checklist

At collection, ask about food, drinking, toileting, medication and any behavior or health changes. Bring home the care record and remaining supplies. Use an appropriate [car-travel setup](/pet-guides/dog-car-travel-safety) for both journeys.

If concerns develop after the stay, contact your veterinarian and mention the boarding dates and any reported symptoms. The value of preparation is continuity: the next carer should not have to reconstruct what happened from memory.
